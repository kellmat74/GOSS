## When does this come up?

The Soviet player has accumulated First Strike Points from card play or end-of-turn SSBN payload checks (9.1.1) and chooses to spend one for a powerful in-game effect. FSPs are spent during the Soviet player's own action — does not count as an action and costs no OPS (4.2.2.1).

## Procedure

1. On your action, declare you are spending one or more First Strike Points (4.2.2.1).
2. Reduce the First Strike Points marker on the track by the appropriate amount.
3. Apply the chosen FSP effect per 9.1.1.
   {{PA-BLOCK: pa8:first-strike-points — First Strike Points (spending options)}}
4. Play continues — this is not an action, so the next action belongs to you (or your opponent under the standard alternation).

## Coach

The 2-FSP ASAT spend is the most under-rated lever, it removes 1 OPS from every NATO card for the turn (9.1.1) and only clears on a 6+ end-of-turn roll. Save toward the 5-FSP battlefield nukes spend when you need to move a Front 2 boxes; that typically demands 3-4 turns of accumulation. The First Strike event card cannot be played from hand via +2 OPS (9.1.1), the +2 itself counts as spending points.

## Why and what to watch for

First Strike Points are the Soviet nuclear-posture currency. They are earned by event card play and at the end of First Strike turns when on-station SSBN Payload value is high enough (4-7 = 1 FSP, 8+ = 2 FSP, per the FIRST STRIKE card clarification) (9.1.1). Spending one is free of OPS and not an action, it's a "use when active" lever that converts strategic credibility into immediate game effect (4.2.2.1).

The spending menu is non-obvious and deserves serious thought before you spend (9.1.1):
- **1 FSP, Strategic Reserve step**: take one step from the strategic reserve. Cheap reinforcement, but worth more later in the game than early.
- **1-2 FSP, ASAT system**: 1 in box prevents NATO Whitecloud and Snoopers cards and applies DRM to NATO detection; 2 in boxes additionally removes one OPS from each NATO card. Removes on 6+ at end of turn. This is the most under-rated FSP use, degrades NATO economy for the entire turn at 2 FSP.
- **3 FSP, Nukes at sea**: trigger naval nuclear weapons use. Game-shaping; usually reserved for desperate situations.
- **5 FSP, Battlefield nukes**: move a Front 2 boxes (reduces VLs to 1 H&S), shifts Soviet stability 1 right, prevents NATO use of nukes. This is the war-winning spend.
- **2-4 FSP, Prevent NATO Ruhr nukes**: 2 to prevent, 4 if chemicals already used. Defensive parity move.

The gotcha is the Ruhr re-trigger and accounting. The Soviet First Strike event card with "Play before spending any points" cannot be played from hand via the +2 OPS rule (9.1.1), doing so means you've already spent points to play it (2 OPS counts as spending). Also note that FSPs are not infinite, you accumulate ~1-2 per turn from SSBN survival plus card play, so a 5-FSP battlefield spend typically demands 3-4 turns of accumulation.

This pairs with [Use a Soviet Spy](./act-active-soviet-spy.md) for layered Soviet pressure: spies disrupt NATO ASW and detection, FSPs degrade NATO posture or trigger nuclear effects. Counter: NATO needs to kill SSBN Payload value on the Nuclear Strike Capability Track (9.1.2) to push Soviet stability rolls, and NATO event cards directly reduce FSPs (e.g. Politburo Interference removes one at end of turn).

Example: turn 4, Soviet sits at 4 FSP after a successful First Strike turn. Soviet player declares FSP spend on their action: 2 FSP into ASAT boxes, NATO loses 1 OPS per card for the entire turn (NATO drops from average ~5 OPS per card to ~4). End of turn each ASAT FSP has a 50% chance to clear, but the turn's already been degraded.
