## When does this come up?

Each turn each player will play two cards for OPS (4.2.2.3). Each card has one event printed on it — playable for the event's printed OPS cost. The cards you used for OPS this turn become available as event cards on your action. Either card's event may be played when active (and does not count as an action by itself, though it does cost OPS).

## Procedure

1. On your action, declare you are playing one of your two OPS cards' (non-Reaction) events (4.2.2.3).
   {{PA-BLOCK: pa2:use-when-active — Use When Active (not an action)}}
2. Pay the event's printed OPS cost from your remaining OPS pool.
3. Resolve the event per the card's text.
4. Each card can be used only once per turn unless the event text specifies otherwise.

## Coach

Fire the event when its printed cost beats the OPS you would otherwise spend to replicate the effect, free strikes, free detection, and anti-tech effects are the obvious wins. Each event can only fire once per turn unless the text says otherwise (4.2.2.3), and the card goes to discard at turn end whether you used it or not. Watch for opponent reaction cards that may interrupt your event resolution (4.2.3.1).

## Why and what to watch for

Each turn each player plays two cards face-up for their printed OPS value (4.2.2.3). The card text, both ops event and reaction event, remains available throughout that turn at its printed OPS cost. Playing an event during your action is "use when active": free of an action slot but still costs OPS. The design models the dual nature of these cards as both an economic resource (OPS for actions) and a strategic intervention (events).

Take this when the event's printed OPS cost is less than what you'd otherwise spend to achieve the same effect. Many ops events bypass standard procedures: free strikes, free detection, free reinforcements, anti-tech effects. If a card's event costs 2 OPS and would otherwise require 4+ OPS of actions to replicate, the math is obvious. If the event is purely flavorful or marginal, leave it on the table, your remaining OPS go further in standard actions.

The timing gotcha is that each event can only be used once per turn unless the text explicitly says otherwise (4.2.2.3), so playing it early commits you. Either of your two OPS-card events can be played at any point during the turn until it's used. There is no "save the event for next turn", at end of turn the card goes to discard regardless. The other timing trade-off is the +2 OPS hand-card route (4.2.2.5) versus revealing here: cards played for OPS reveal their event publicly; cards in hand stay hidden until used.

This pairs with the card hand as a whole, if you have a strong event in hand that pairs with a different OPS card already played, you can fire both in the same turn (the hand-card version costs +2 OPS and reveals on play). Counter: opponent's reaction cards (4.2.3.1) interrupt your event resolution if your event triggers their reaction (Submarine Pairing on step loss, Distant Support on adjacent strike, etc.).

Example: NATO plays "F-14 Wing" for OPS this turn (3 OPS face value) and now declares the event from that card to grant F-14 anti-missile +1 DRM in an upcoming strike defense. Event cost is 1 OPS, paid from remaining pool. Resolves immediately. The card stays on the table marked Used; the other OPS card's event is still available later this turn.
