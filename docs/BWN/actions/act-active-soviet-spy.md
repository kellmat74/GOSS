## When does this come up?

The Soviet player has spies remaining on the Soviet Spies Track and chooses to spend one. Spies provide a menu of effects (place a Poor Detection on a NATO TF, take the SSBN Hunting marker, re-roll up to 3 ASW dice, or auto-succeed on First Strike sub evasion in a zone) (9.3).

## Procedure

1. On your action, declare you are spending a Soviet spy (4.2.2.2).
2. Choose one effect from 9.3:
   - Place a Poor Detection marker on any NATO Task Force.
   - Take the SSBN Hunting marker.
   - Re-roll up to three ASW dice rolled (one re-roll per unit maximum).
   - Automatically succeed on all First Strike submarine evasion rolls in a single zone.
   {{PA-BLOCK: pa8:soviet-spies — Soviet Spies (effects menu)}}
3. Apply the chosen effect immediately.
4. **Infiltration check**: roll a die and reference the current box on the Soviet Spy Infiltration Track. If the die falls within the box's indicated range, a spy has been caught — move the track down one box (to the left). This may reduce future spy availability.

## Why and what to watch for

Soviet Spies are a finite, depleting resource on the Spy Infiltration Track that converts to one of four effects per use (9.3). The design models Soviet covert intelligence networks doing things that no in-game unit can: placing arbitrary Poor Detection markers on NATO TFs, claiming the SSBN Hunting marker without combat, re-rolling ASW dice, and auto-succeeding SSBN evasion. The active version (this action) is the proactive use; 4.2.3.5 is the reactive evasion version.

Take this when one of the four effects substantially changes a turn's math: a Poor Detection on a known NATO TF unlocks Soviet missile/strike capability against it that turn (Good is required for missiles, but Poor seeds the upgrade chain); SSBN Hunting marker grabbed without combat avoids the natural-10 dependency; ASW re-rolls (up to 3, one per unit) salvage a critical sub hunt that whiffed. Spend earlier-game when the infiltration track is healthier (less catch risk) and effects matter most.

The gotcha is the infiltration check (9.3): after every use, roll a die against the current box's range on the Soviet Spy Infiltration Track. If the die falls within the range, a spy is caught and the track shifts one box left, reducing future spy availability. The KGB Assassination card requires a spy to spend even though it has its own OPS cost. Spies on the track count: when you're out, you're out, no card-free way to replenish. The board "Spies" Track in the top-left of the map is the source of truth. And note this is NOT the same action as [Soviet Spy for SSBN evasion](./act-anytime-spy-fs-evasion.md), which is reactive during a NATO ASW attack.

This pairs with the Soviet detection chain, spy-placed Poor Detection plus MP follow-up plus RORSAT coverage is how Soviet builds reliable Good Detection on NATO TFs. Pair with [Spend FSP](./act-active-fsp.md) for layered Soviet leverage in the same turn. Counter: NATO cards that target spy infrastructure, and time spent attacking Lourdes / pressuring Cuba reduce Soviet spy support indirectly. NATO cannot directly "catch" spies on a turn-by-turn basis, only the infiltration roll does that.

Example: Soviet declares spy on their action, chooses Place Poor Detection on a NATO carrier TF in East 4 (previously undetected). Marker placed. Roll infiltration check against current box range (say 1-2 because the track has been used). Rolls a 4, no catch. Soviet now has a Poor on the NATO carrier, immediately follows up next action with an MP to upgrade to Good for missile salvos.
