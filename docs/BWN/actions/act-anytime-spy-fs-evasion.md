## When does this come up?

During a NATO action that would resolve First Strike submarine evasion against a Soviet SSBN, the Soviet player may spend a spy to automatically succeed at all evasion rolls in a single zone (even after rolling). This is a reactive use of the spy menu (4.2.3.5, 9.3).

## Procedure

1. During NATO's First Strike submarine evasion resolution, declare you are spending a Soviet spy (4.2.3.5).
2. Apply the 9.3 effect: automatically succeed on all First Strike submarine evasion rolls in a single zone (even if you have already rolled and failed).
   {{PA-BLOCK: pa8:soviet-spies — Soviet Spies (effects menu)}}
   {{PA-BLOCK: pa8:soviet-first-strike-submarines — Soviet First Strike Submarines (the evasion this saves)}}
3. **Infiltration check**: roll a die against the current box on the Soviet Spy Infiltration Track. If the die falls within the box's range, a spy has been caught — move the track down one box (left) (9.3).

## Coach

The killer feature: this works AFTER ROLLING (9.3), so let NATO commit and roll evasion before deciding to burn the spy. Reserve for high-Payload boomers (Typhoon, Delta IV) where the FSP and Nuclear Strike Capability Track math (9.1.1, 9.1.2) outweighs the infiltration risk; don't burn it on a 1-step Yankee. Every spy use rolls an infiltration check that can shift the track left, so each reactive save compounds future spy scarcity.

## Why and what to watch for

This is the reactive spy use: NATO is rolling First Strike submarine evasion against a Soviet SSBN, and the Soviet player burns a spy to auto-succeed all evasion rolls in that zone (4.2.3.5, 9.3). The design models Soviet covert intelligence diverting NATO ASW efforts at the critical moment when an SSBN is under attack. Crucially this works EVEN AFTER ROLLING, if you've already rolled the SSBN evasion and failed, spend the spy and the evasion succeeds anyway.

Take this when an SSBN with significant Payload value is about to be sunk. End-of-turn SSBN tally drives First Strike Points (9.1.1) and Nuclear Strike Capability Track moves (9.1.2). A Typhoon's 8-point Payload disappearing from on-station is worth multiple FSPs over the game's remaining turns; spending a spy to keep it alive is high-value math when you're at the boomer-protection inflection point. Conversely, don't burn a spy to save a 1-step Yankee whose Payload is already discounted in 1989.

The gotcha is the infiltration check: after every spy use you roll a die against the current box on the Soviet Spy Infiltration Track (9.3). If the die falls within the box's range, a spy has been caught, the track moves one box left, reducing future spy availability. Reactive spy use looks free in OPS but compounds the eventual loss of spies over the game. Also note the regular spy menu (9.3) is the proactive version, placing detection, taking the SSBN Hunting marker, ASW re-rolls, but this specific reaction is the one that activates DURING a NATO ASW attack, not before.

This pairs with [Use a Soviet Spy](./act-active-soviet-spy.md) (the proactive cousin) and Soviet SSBN positioning (2.3.5.1), keep SSBNs in zones where evasion is plausibly possible so the spy save has somewhere to send them. The standard evasion roll is 6+ (8+ if spent) and moves the SSBN plus one non-diesel escort to an adjacent zone (2.3.5.1). Counter: NATO can try to drown evasion rolls by stacking multiple ASW attacks in the same turn before the Soviet has rolled, but a spy save still auto-succeeds even after rolling, so this rarely helps.

Example: NATO P-3 attacks a Soviet Typhoon in the Bastion. Soviet rolls 6+ evasion, gets a 3, failed. NATO begins resolving the ASW hits. Soviet player declares spy reaction (4.2.3.5), spending one spy from the Spies Track: evasion auto-succeeds. Typhoon and a non-diesel escort move to an adjacent zone, attacker is Spent regardless of targets. Soviet then rolls infiltration check against current box range (say 1-3), rolls a 2, spy caught, track shifts one box left.
