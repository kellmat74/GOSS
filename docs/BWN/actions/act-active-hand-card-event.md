## When does this come up?

You want to fire a (non-Reaction) event from a card sitting in your hand — one you have NOT spent on OPS this turn. This is allowed at a premium: pay 2 OPS surcharge on top of the event's printed cost (4.2.2.5).

## Procedure

1. On your action, declare you are playing the top (non-Reaction) event from a card in your hand (4.2.2.5).
   {{PA-BLOCK: pa2:use-when-active — Use When Active (not an action)}}
2. Pay the event's printed OPS cost **plus** 2 additional OPS.
3. Resolve the event per the card text.
4. Place the card in the discard pile, or remove it from play if the event instructs you to.
5. If your hand drops below three cards as a result, draw up to three (4.2.3.6).

## Coach

Reserve the +2 OPS surcharge (4.2.2.5) for events the opponent would specifically play around if revealed, KGB Assassination, Surge, hidden-timing kills. Otherwise just play the card for OPS and fire the event normally. Reactions cannot use this route (they fire free on trigger, 4.2.3.1), and the Soviet First Strike card explicitly cannot be played from hand because +2 OPS itself counts as spending points (9.1.1). Hand drops below three trigger a redraw (4.2.3.6).

## Why and what to watch for

Playing a top (non-Reaction) event from your hand instead of from your face-up OPS cards costs a +2 OPS surcharge on top of the event's printed cost (4.2.2.5). The design intent is explicit: keep events hidden in hand to use at a time of your choosing, at a premium. The hidden-information value is the entire point, your opponent doesn't know you have the card until you fire it.

Take this when the event is critical to a particular turn but you didn't want to reveal it by playing the card for OPS. Common cases: a powerful one-shot that the opponent would specifically play around if they knew it was coming (KGB Assassination, Surge, certain Fast events that ignore Day 2), or an event whose timing matters more than its OPS cost. The +2 OPS surcharge is real, a 2-OPS event from hand costs 4 OPS to fire, which is most of an average card's value, so reserve this for events that matter strategically.

The gotcha is the cost stack. 2 OPS surcharge plus the printed event cost can easily approach a full card's OPS budget for a single event. There's no way to play a Reaction event from hand via this route, Reactions are always free-to-play on trigger (4.2.3.1). Also note that the Soviet First Strike event card with "Play before spending any points" cannot be played from hand via the +2 OPS rule (9.1.1 clarification) because the +2 OPS itself constitutes spending points. If your hand drops below three cards after the play, draw back up to three (4.2.3.6), which is a small upside if you were holding too many cards.

This pairs with hidden-info play: keeping a critical event in hand all game means the opponent can never plan around it. Counter: there's no direct counter except draining the opponent's OPS through aggressive play, making the +2 surcharge unaffordable. Sometimes opponents force a card play earlier with timing pressure.

Example: NATO has been holding "Convoy Re-route" in hand all game (a hypothetical card). On turn 5, with the Atlantic convoy lane heavily threatened, NATO declares the hand-card event from active action: event printed cost 1 OPS + 2 OPS surcharge = 3 OPS. NATO had 5 OPS remaining; now 2 OPS remain. The convoy re-routes and the Soviet sub layer is bypassed for this turn. Card goes to discard, NATO is at 2 hand cards, draws back to 3 (4.2.3.6).
